Assessment in Units 1 and 2

The rules

All assessment is school-based and the procedures are a school decision. Students must show achievement of all three outcomes in each unit. Assessment should be part of normal teaching, done mainly in class and in limited time. Where students choose between tasks, the tasks must be of similar scope and demand.

Unit 2 Outcome 3 must be assessed by a report of the investigation: a poster, practical report, oral or digital presentation.

Task types allowed (Outcomes 1 to 3 in Unit 1; Outcomes 1 and 2 in Unit 2)

  • report of a lab or fieldwork activity that generates primary data
  • reflective annotations of practicals in the logbook
  • analysis and evaluation of primary and/or secondary data
  • critique of an experimental design, process or apparatus
  • modelling or simulation activity
  • design, build, test and evaluate a device
  • explanation of a physics device, design or innovation
  • physics-referenced response to an issue
  • report on a physics phenomenon
  • media analysis or response
  • infographic
  • problem-solving with physics concepts and skills
  • report applying physics to a real-world context
  • analysis with calculations in real-world contexts
  • comparison and evaluation of two solutions, explanations or methods
  • scientific poster

Practical work

Practical work can include lab experiments, fieldwork, simulations and modelling. Pracs are one lesson each (decided 18 Nov).

Looking ahead: Units 3 and 4 (Year 12 tab)

Unit 3 · How do fields explain motion and electricity?

AoS 1 motion in two dimensions, AoS 2 fields (gravitational, electric, magnetic), AoS 3 electricity generation. 3 SAC tasks, 40 marks each. 30% of the study score.

Unit 4 · How have creative ideas and investigation revolutionised thinking in physics?

AoS 1 how understanding of light, matter and motion has changed (including special relativity), AoS 2 student-designed investigation reported as a scientific poster (max 600 words) plus logbook. 2 tasks, 40 marks each. 20% of the study score.

Exam

One end-of-year exam, 2.5 hours, on all Units 3 and 4 key knowledge and key science skills. 50% of the study score.

Conditions

Each SAC task type can be used only once across Units 3 and 4. Written tasks about 50 minutes; oral or multimodal about 5 minutes. At least 15 h of practical work in Unit 3; in Unit 4, 5 h in AoS 1 and 10 h for the investigation.

Flags for planning

Units 1 and 2 assessment is a school decision. No SAC types or marks are set by VCAA, but every outcome must be assessed for an S, and tasks should mainly be done in class in limited time.
Unit 2 options are a student choice. Cambridge only supports 11 of the 18. Offering a short list (e.g. 3 or 4 options) keeps it manageable; Pearson on the drive adds 2.2, 2.5 and 2.12.
Unit 2 AoS 1 needs a case study (motion in sport, vehicle safety, a device or a structure). Check whether Cambridge Ch 7 to 9 includes one.
Logbooks are required in every unit for satisfactory completion. Teachers must sight them regularly, especially for Unit 2 Outcomes 2 and 3.
Practice exam check: the Cambridge practice exam's DC motor question is Unit 3 content (AoS 2 fields). Remove it before using the paper for Unit 1 or 2.
Group work: investigations may be done in groups, but anything assessed must be completed individually.
