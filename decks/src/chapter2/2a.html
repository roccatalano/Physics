Thermal energy and temperature
Thermal energy: the total random kinetic
and potential energy of all the particles.
Temperature $T$: measures the average
kinetic energy of the particles.
Gain thermal energy: temperature rises.
Lose thermal energy: temperature falls.
Temperature scales
$T\ \text{in K} = T\ \text{in}\ ^\circ\text{C} + 273$
Absolute zero: 0 K = −273 °C.
The particles stop. Nothing is colder.
$\Delta T$ = change in temperature.
A change of 1 K = a change of 1 °C.
Heat and conduction
Heat: thermal energy flowing from a
hotter object to a colder one.
Conduction: particles pass vibrations
to their neighbours. Main way in solids.
Conductors: metals (free electrons).
Insulators: air, wood, plastic.
Heat flows until the temperatures are
equal: thermal equilibrium.
Worked example
Liquid nitrogen at 77 K warms to 22 °C.
Find its change in temperature.
V$T_1 = 77$ K, $T_2 = 22$ °C
F$\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(\Delta T\)} = \colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(T_2\)} - T_1$
$\colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(T_2\)} = 22 + 273 = \colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(295\)}$ K
S$\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(\Delta T\)} = \colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(295\)} - 77$
S$\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(\Delta T\)} = 218$ K
The temperature rises by 218 K.

F$\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(\Delta T\)} = T_2 - \colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(T_1\)}$

S$\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(\Delta T\)} = 105$ K
The temperature rises by 105 K.

Convection
Convection: a liquid or gas moves and
carries thermal energy with it.
Warm fluid is less dense, so it rises.
Cool fluid is denser, so it sinks.
Radiation
Radiation: energy carried by EM waves,
mostly infrared. It needs no particles.
Everything above 0 K gives off radiation.
Black, matt: absorb and emit well.
Shiny, white: reflect.
Evaporative cooling
The fastest surface particles escape.
Those left have less kinetic energy on
average, so the liquid cools.
Faster with a breeze or a bigger surface.

Specific heat capacity
Specific heat capacity $c$: the energy
needed to warm 1 kg by 1 K.
$Q$ = energy (J), $m$ = mass (kg)
$c$ = specific heat capacity (J/(kg K))
$\Delta T$ = change in temperature (K)
$Q = mc\Delta T$
Water: $c$ = 4190 J/(kg K)
Copper: $c$ = 385 J/(kg K)
High $c$: slow to heat up, slow to cool.
Changing state
While the state changes, the temperature
stays the same. The energy breaks bonds.
Specific latent heat $L$: the energy to
change the state of 1 kg (J/kg).
$Q = mL$
Melting or freezing: $L_{\text{fusion}}$
Boiling or condensing: $L_{\text{vaporisation}}$
Water: $L_{\text{fusion}}$ = 334 kJ/kg
Water: $L_{\text{vaporisation}}$ = 2256 kJ/kg
Worked example
How much energy heats 250 g of water
from 18 °C to 95 °C?
V$m = 0.250$ kg, $c = 4190$ J/(kg K)
18 °C to 95 °C
F$\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(Q\)} = mc\,\colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(\Delta T\)}$
$\colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(\Delta T\)} = 95 - 18 = \colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(77\)}$ K
S$\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(Q\)} = 0.250 \times 4190 \times \colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(77\)}$
S$\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(Q\)} = 80\,658 \approx 8.1 \times 10^{4}$ J
It takes $8.1 \times 10^{4}$ J, or 81 kJ.

Heating ice to steam
Sloping parts: $Q = mc\Delta T$
Flat parts: $Q = mL$
Total: add the energy for each part.
! Convert kJ/kg to J/kg before you add.

$+\ 0.20 \times 4190 \times 25$
S$\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(Q\)} = 66\,800 + 20\,950 = 87\,750$ J
It takes $8.8 \times 10^{4}$ J, or 88 kJ.
