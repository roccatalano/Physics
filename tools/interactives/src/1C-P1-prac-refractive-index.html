    How close?

    Write these in your log book, in your own words

    1. Aim. One sentence.
      To find the refractive index of an unknown material from a graph of sin θ1 against sin θ2.
    2. Results. Your table and graph. Print the results page and glue it in.
    3. The ray. What happened to it as it went from air into the block?
      It slowed and bent towards the normal. A faint ray reflected off the flat face. It left the curved face without bending, because it travelled along a radius.
    4. Why the sines? Why not just graph θ1 against θ2?
      Snell's law says sin θ1 = n sin θ2. The sines are proportional, so they give a straight line through the origin with gradient n. The angles are not proportional: their graph curves.
    5. Sources of error. Name two and say what each did to your readings.
      The ray is about 2° wide, so its middle is hard to judge. The block may not sit exactly on the centre. The scale can only be read to about half a degree, and θ1 is set by eye.
    6. One improvement and why it helps.
      A narrower slit gives a thinner ray. Repeats and averages reduce random error. A larger protractor spreads the degrees further apart.
