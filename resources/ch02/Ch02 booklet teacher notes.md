## For you to decide

### Worksheet 2A Q1 (booklet page 5): "Fusion" matched to "change from liquid to solid"
In standard physics, in the study design and in Cambridge's own Chapter 2 checklist (2A.6, "fusion (melting)"), fusion means melting. The textbook's own margin glossary (2A, beside "Energy required to change state") defines fusion as "the change of state from liquid to solid", and the worksheet follows it: Melting goes to a, Fusion goes to d. So the worksheet is consistent with the textbook, but the textbook disagrees with the study design. I did not put this on the Corrections page. Suggest telling the class: the latent heat of fusion is the same number for melting and freezing, and on a VCAA paper "fusion" means melting.

## Errors and weak spots in Cambridge's answers

### Worksheet 2B Q1 answer table (booklet page 9)
- Row ii (Back radiation) carries the note "these wavelengths readily escape through the atmosphere to space". That note belongs to row i (atmospheric window). For back radiation it is wrong: back radiation is IR sent from the atmosphere back down to the surface.
- Row ix note gives Wien's law as λmax T = 2.989 × 10^-3 m K. The constant is 2.898 × 10^-3 m K (the study design rounds to 2.90 × 10^-3 m K). The digits are swapped.
- Row vi prints "IPPC"; the worksheet correctly says IPCC.
- The matches themselves are right: i g, ii c, iii e, iv a, v b, vi j, vii i, viii f, ix d, x h.

### Worksheet 2A Q8a (booklet page 7): solar radiation percentages
Cambridge's answer is about 4% UV, 54% IR and 42% visible at the surface. These are fair surface figures, but they are not in the textbook. The textbook gives 48% IR, 44% visible, 7% UV (Chapter 1 text under Figure 1B-4; the figure itself labels 7% UV, 43% visible, 38% near IR and 11% far IR) and, in 2A, "93% infrared and visible and 7% ultraviolet". Students working from the book will give those numbers. Suggest accepting either set.

### Worksheet 2A Q9c (booklet page 7): sign of the energy change
Working: Q1 = mcΔT = 0.500 × 4190 × 20 = 41 900 J = 41.9 kJ. Q2 = mL = 0.500 × 334 = 167 kJ. Total = 41.9 + 167 = 208.9 kJ, about 209 kJ. Cambridge's 209 kJ is right, but the question asks for the "thermal energy change" and the water loses this energy. Suggest the answer says "209 kJ released" (or -209 kJ). Cambridge also adds the rounded 42 kJ rather than 41.9 kJ; the result is the same to 3 significant figures.

### Worksheet 2B Q2c (booklet page 10): Sun and Earth
Cambridge says the Sun's kelvin temperature is "about 22" times Earth's. With the textbook's own values, 5778 / 288 = 20.1, so "about 20". The reasoning (peak wavelength is inversely proportional to kelvin temperature) is right. The answer also has a typo, "Wien' law".

### Worksheet 2A Q4 (booklet page 6)
Cambridge links temperature to "the average energy of the molecules". Strictly it is the average random (translational) kinetic energy; potential energy is not part of it. Worth saying, as that is exactly the difference the question asks about.

### Worksheet 2A Q3 (booklet page 6): firewalk
The textbook (Figure 2A-5 caption) names two factors: low thermal conductivity and low specific heat of charcoal. Cambridge's answer adds a third, short contact time. All three are reasonable; the question asks for three.
