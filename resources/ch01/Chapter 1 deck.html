Waves
A wave carries energy, not matter.
Transverse: vibration at right angles to travel. e.g. light, ripples
Longitudinal: vibration along the direction of travel. e.g. sound
Measuring a wave
$A$ = amplitude (m)
$\lambda$ = wavelength (m)
$T$ = period: time for one wave (s)
$f$ = frequency: waves per second (Hz)
The wave equation
$v$ = wave speed (m/s)
$v = f\lambda$$\lambda = \dfrac{v}{f}$$f = \dfrac{1}{T}$
Medium sets $v$. Source sets $f$.
$\lambda$ is the result.
! Shaking faster does NOT make the wave travel faster.
Worked example

$\colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(f\)} = \dfrac{8}{5.0} = \colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(1.6\)}$ Hz
S$\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(v\)} = \colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(1.6\)} \times 2.5$
S$\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(v\)} = 4.0$ m/s
The waves travel at 4.0 m/s.

Electromagnetic waves
EM wave: vibrating electric (E) and magnetic (B) fields.
Transverse: the fields vibrate at right angles to the direction of travel.
Needs no medium. Sound does.
In a vacuum, all EM waves travel at the speed of light, $c$:
$c = 3.00 \times 10^{8}$ m/s
The EM spectrum
$c = f\lambda$
$c$ is the same for all, so a shorter $\lambda$ means a higher $f$.
Visible: 400 nm (violet) to 700 nm (red)
Uses
Radio: FM radio, TV, MRI scans
Microwave: wi-fi, radar, ovens
Infrared: remotes, thermal cameras
Visible: sight, photosynthesis
Ultraviolet: vitamin D, killing germs
X-ray: bone images, airport scanners
Gamma: cancer treatment, sterilising
Long $\lambda$: low energy, safe. Used to communicate.
Short $\lambda$: high energy. Can damage cells.
Sunlight
Mainly infrared (49%), visible (43%) and ultraviolet (7%).
Prefixes
G (giga) $10^{9}$   M (mega) $10^{6}$   k (kilo) $10^{3}$
c (centi) $10^{-2}$   m (milli) $10^{-3}$
μ (micro) $10^{-6}$   n (nano) $10^{-9}$
! Change to Hz and m before you use $c = f\lambda$.

Refraction
Refraction: light changes direction
because it changes speed.
Into slower: bends towards the normal.
Into faster: bends away from the normal.
Refractive index
$n$ = refractive index (no unit)
$v$ = speed of light in the material (m/s)
$c$ = $3.00 \times 10^8$ m/s, light in a vacuum
$n = \dfrac{c}{v}$$n_1v_1 = n_2v_2$
Bigger $n$ = slower light.
e.g. air 1.00, water 1.33, glass 1.50
$f$ stays the same. $\lambda$ gets shorter.
Snell's law
$\theta$ = angle between ray and normal
1 = first material, 2 = second material
$n_1\sin\theta_1 = n_2\sin\theta_2$

Light in diamond: $1.24 \times 10^8$ m/s.
Find the critical angle from diamond
into air.
V$v_1 = 1.24 \times 10^8$ m/s, $n_2 = 1.00$
F$\colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(n_1\)}\sin\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(\theta_c\)} = n_2$
$\colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(n_1\)} = \dfrac{3.00 \times 10^8}{1.24 \times 10^8} = \colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(2.42\)}$
S$\colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(2.42\)} \times \sin\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(\theta_c\)} = 1.00$
S$\sin\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(\theta_c\)} = \dfrac{1.00}{\colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(2.42\)}} = 0.413$
$\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(\theta_c\)} = 24.4°$
The critical angle for diamond is 24.4°.
You try
Light in a glass: $2.00 \times 10^8$ m/s.
Find the critical angle from this glass
into air.
V$v_1 = 2.00 \times 10^8$ m/s, $n_2 = 1.00$
F$\colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(n_1\)}\sin\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(\theta_c\)} = n_2$
$\colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(n_1\)} = \dfrac{3.00 \times 10^8}{2.00 \times 10^8} = \colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(1.50\)}$
S$\colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(1.50\)} \times \sin\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(\theta_c\)} = 1.00$
S$\sin\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(\theta_c\)} = \dfrac{1.00}{\colorbox{#b9f5b0}{\(1.50\)}} = 0.667$
$\colorbox{#ffee58}{\(\theta_c\)} = 41.8°$
The critical angle is 41.8°.
Dispersion
Dispersion: white light splits into colours.
In glass, $n$ depends on the colour.
Violet: biggest $n$, bends most. Red: least.
Chromatic distortion: a lens focuses
each colour at a different point.
Rainbows and mirages
Rainbow: in each raindrop, light refracts
in, reflects off the back and refracts out.
See it with the Sun behind you.
Mirage: hot air near a road has a
lower $n$. Light from the sky bends
back up into your eye.
