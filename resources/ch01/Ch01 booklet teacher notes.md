## Errors and weak spots in Cambridge's answers

### Worksheet 1B Q1 (booklet page 7): spectrum diagram
Cambridge's answer pairs 10^24 Hz (gamma) with 10^-17 m. From c = fλ, λ = 3.0 × 10^8 / 10^24 = 3 × 10^-16 m, so the wavelength label should be about 10^-16 m. The other pairs are consistent to an order of magnitude (for example 10^14 Hz gives 3 × 10^-6 m, labelled 10^-6 m; strictly that is infrared, as visible light is about 4 × 10^-7 m to 7 × 10^-7 m). Accept order-of-magnitude labels.

### Worksheet 1B Q6a (booklet page 8): "UV/X-ray range"
f = 3.0 × 10^17 Hz gives λ = 3.0 × 10^8 / 3.0 × 10^17 = 1.0 × 10^-9 m = 1 nm (Cambridge's own Q6b). 1 nm is X-ray (UV ends at about 10 nm, about 3 × 10^16 Hz). The answer should be X-ray; "UV/X-ray" hedges.

### Worksheet 1B Q5 (booklet page 8): GPS
Cambridge suggests microwaves and infrared. GPS signals are already at 1.2 GHz to 1.6 GHz, which is microwave (the question calls them radio waves, which is fair in the broad sense). Infrared is a poor suggestion: water vapour and cloud absorb and scatter it, so it would not reach receivers reliably from orbit. Suggest accepting microwaves with a reason (passes through the atmosphere and cloud) and not crediting infrared without a sensible limitation.

### Worksheet 1B Q4 (booklet page 7)
The answer is only "Similar answer to Q3." Expected: radio waves (as used by real deep space probes); they cross huge distances through space with little absorption and are harmless.

### Worksheet 1C Q1 (booklet page 9): why light is slower in water
Cambridge says light "scatters the molecules" and slows more in a "denser" medium, and adds that sound is faster in denser media. This is loose physics:
- Light slows because it interacts with the charges in the material (the material's electrons respond to the wave and the combined wave travels more slowly). It is not scattering, and the photons do not need a medium.
- The relevant idea is optical density (refractive index), not mass density. Some less dense materials have a higher refractive index than denser ones (oil floats on water but has n of about 1.47).
- Sound speed depends on stiffness as well as density; for the same stiffness a denser medium is slower.
Suggested marking: credit "light interacts with the particles of the water, which slows it" and "water has a higher refractive index, n = c/v = 3.0 / 2.25 = 1.33".

### Worksheet 1C Q2 (booklet page 9): why the waves change direction
Cambridge's answer reverses cause and effect: it says the wavelength changes because the media have different densities, "therefore" the velocity changes. The speed changes because the medium changes; the frequency stays the same, so the wavelength changes (λ = v/f). Because the wavefront meets the boundary at an angle, one side slows first, which turns the wavefront. Suggested marking: speed changes in the new medium (1), one part of the wavefront changes speed before the rest, so the direction changes (1).

### Worksheet 1C Q3c (booklet page 10): angle of incidence 50°
Cambridge says the refraction angle "is greater than 90°". Working: sin θ2 = 1.34 × sin 50° = 1.34 × 0.766 = 1.026. A sine cannot exceed 1, so there is no refracted ray at all. The critical angle is sin^-1(1 / 1.34) = 48.3°, and 50° > 48.3°, so total internal reflection occurs. Cambridge's conclusion is right; the wording is not.
