Key ideas

The properties of waves

Definitions

Transverse wave: a wave in which vibrations are perpendicular to the direction of travel
Longitudinal wave: a wave in which vibrations are parallel to the direction of travel
Amplitude, A: a measure of the size of vibrations in a wave as the maximum distance a point in the wave vibrates away from the neutral position; generally measured in metres (m)
Wavelength, λ: the distance between repeated parts of a wave shape; measured in metres (m)
Period, T: the time it takes for a wave to move one wavelength in the direction of travel; measured in seconds (s)

Year 9 · 9.5 Matter and energy: waves
A wave carries energy from one place to another. Every wave has a frequency and an amplitude.
A swimmer floats as waves pass. Does the swimmer travel to the beach with the waves?
No. The swimmer bobs up and down while the energy passes by.
Year 9 · 9.6 Energy transfer by sound
Sound is a pressure wave: it needs particles to travel through.
Why can't sound travel through space?
Space is almost empty. There are no particles to pass the vibration on.

New knowledgeThe nature of waves (transverse and longitudinal)

The properties of waves

If you look carefully at the movement of the spring, you will notice that although the wave moves to the left, the individual coils of the spring do not move left (or right); they just vibrate up and down.

Waves like this are called transverse waves.

Another everyday example of transverse waves are water waves, such as ocean swells or ripples in a pond where the water particles move more or less up and down as the wave moves along.

There are other kinds of waves where the particles vibrate parallel to the direction of travel of the wave; these are called longitudinal waves.
