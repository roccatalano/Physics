The design: one section

Every section follows the same pattern. Check-ins are spread through the theory, not left to the end.

Learning objectives at the start of each chapter come straight from the study design. They return at the end as the checklist's success criteria, each linked to the review questions that assess it. Units close with a revision exercise; the course closes with a practice exam.

Every resource type

Section

Check-in questions

What it is. Short sets of 3 to 4 questions placed straight after each chunk of theory inside a section, so a section has one to five sets.

What Cambridge says it is for. Cambridge calls these formative assessment: a quick check that the chunk just taught has landed before moving on.

On the drive. 63 sets across Unit 1, each its own Word file. Answers are in "Chapter N Suggested responses" (TEACHER ONLY).

Planning note. Natural fit for checking understanding mid-lesson, or as retrieval questions later. Short and low-stakes.

Section

Worked examples

What it is. Boxed, fully solved problems in the textbook, placed right after the theory they apply.

What Cambridge says it is for. Model how to solve the section's calculation and reasoning problems before students try them.

On the drive. 47 in Unit 1, inside the chapter PDFs only (no separate files). Four sections have none: 1B, 3D, 4C and 6C.

Planning note. The backbone of an "I do / we do" step. Sections without them are mostly descriptive.

Section

Skills boxes

What it is. One per section, near the end. Exam technique for the question types in that section, each with a video in the Interactive Textbook.

What Cambridge says it is for. Advice on how to answer and prepare for questions, especially in exams.

On the drive. In the chapter PDFs. The videos are online only.

Planning note. Short teacher talk or a slide before practice. Good exam-habit building from Unit 1.

Section

Section questions

What it is. A set of multiple-choice and short-answer questions at the end of each section.

What Cambridge says it is for. Cambridge calls these summative for the section, with full answers in the digital resources.

On the drive. 21 files (one per section). Answers in "Chapter N Suggested responses".

Planning note. The main independent practice and homework set for a section.

Section

Worksheets

What it is. Printable extra practice for a section, usually marked out of about 15, with matching, calculation and short-answer items.

What Cambridge says it is for. Extra questions and activities, and in some cases extra content. Editable copies are in the teacher resources.

On the drive. 20 in Unit 1 (none for 3B). Answers in TEACHER ONLY.

Planning note. Extension, consolidation, a relief-teacher lesson, or targeted re-teaching after a weak check-in.

Section

Practicals

What it is. A prelab (introduction, aim, prelab questions) followed by the practical (aim, materials, safety notes, method, results, discussion questions with marks).

What Cambridge says it is for. Downloadable, editable and printable practical investigations linked to their section.

On the drive. 14 in Unit 1, clustered in Ch 3 (4), Ch 5 (3) and Ch 6 (3). Ch 4 has one, a simulation. There are no worksheet-style answers for pracs on the drive.

Planning note. These are the obvious source for logbook pracs. Unit 1 needs at least 15 h of practical work, so the 14 need checking against timing.

Section

PowerPoint figures

What it is. Single-figure or table slide shows taken from the book (e.g. Figure 1B-3, Table 3B-1).

What Cambridge says it is for. Animated versions of key charts and diagrams for teachers to project.

On the drive. 33 in Unit 1 section folders plus one in the Ch 4 review folder.

Planning note. Drop-in visuals for slides. They are not lesson decks.

Chapter

Chapter review

What it is. Four parts: a summary task, a checklist of success criteria, then multiple-choice and short-answer review questions.

What Cambridge says it is for. Students write their own summary notes (Cambridge provides a model summary). The checklist lists the chapter's learning objectives as success criteria, each linked to the review questions that test it, so students tick off what they can do.

On the drive. 6 checklists, 6 summaries (model answers), 6 review question sets (Ch 1 has 36 questions), 6 curated internet resource lists.

Planning note. A built-in end-of-chapter revision lesson, and a ready-made success-criteria list. The links between criteria and questions make it easy to target weak spots.

Chapter

Chapter tests

What it is. One editable test per chapter in TEACHER ONLY, multiple choice then short answer, with worked answers at the end of the same file.

What Cambridge says it is for. Editable and printable chapter tests with answers, in the teacher resources.

On the drive. Totals vary a lot: Ch 1 /28, Ch 2 /22, Ch 3 /25, Ch 4 /30, Ch 5 /58, Ch 6 /44.

Planning note. Fits a topic test or CFAT after each chapter. The longer ones would need trimming to fit a single lesson.

Unit / course

Unit revision and practice exam

What it is. A Unit 1 revision exercise (24 questions, with suggested responses), the textbook's revision pages, and a Units 1&2 practice exam (reading 15 min, writing 95 min, Section A 20 MC, Section B 28 short answer, 83 marks) with answers.

What Cambridge says it is for. End-of-unit revision. The practice exam was made by Cambridge's exam generator from VCAA questions.

On the drive. Course-wide folder and Unit 1 Revision folder.

Planning note. Semester exam preparation. Check the practice exam's topic coverage first (see Flags).

What this means for planning

Lesson unit

Cambridge is organised by section, not by lesson. Sections vary from about 2 to 15 pages and 0 to 7 worked examples, so some sections fit one lesson and others need two or three.

Inside a lesson

The textbook's own rhythm is teach a chunk, check-in, teach, check-in, worked example, practise. That maps closely onto explain, check, model, practise.

End of a section

Section questions are the natural independent practice or homework. Worksheets add depth where needed.

End of a chapter

The review is a ready-made revision lesson, the checklist is a ready-made set of success criteria, and the chapter test is a ready-made CFAT or topic test.

Pracs

14 practicals sit inside their sections. Whether they run as a weekly prac lesson or inside the section is a timetabling choice, not something Cambridge prescribes.

Still open

Where do-nows, APK and exit tickets come from? Cambridge's check-ins and section questions can supply them, but it does not label anything as a do-now or exit ticket.

Flags

Online only, not on the drive

The Interactive Textbook videos (concept and skills videos), the "Scorcher" key-term quizzes, the Years 9 and 10 prior-knowledge quiz, Cambridge's teaching programs, teacher notes, the question bank and test generator, and extra assessment tasks all live in the Online Teaching Suite. None are in the shared drive folders. The teaching programs would be worth downloading if the school has access.

Gaps in the section files

3B has no worksheet. 1B, 3D, 4C and 6C have no worked examples. Ch 4 has a single practical and it is a simulation.

Chapter tests are uneven

Totals run from 22 to 58 marks. Ch 5 (58) and Ch 6 (44) are too long for one lesson as written.

Practice exam scope

It was generated from VCAA questions. At least one item (DC motor torque) looks like content outside the 2023 Units 1&2 study design. Check every question before using it as a semester exam.

Corrections

Course-wide\Updates_Feb2023.pdf lists errata for the first print, e.g. 1A Check-in Set 2 Q2 should ask for the wave equation with T as the subject. Apply these before using the affected items.

Odd files

Course-wide holds Physics12_book.pdf (64 MB, looks like a different book) and the Unit 2 investigation folders only contain placeholder README files.

Answers

Check-ins and section questions: "Chapter N Suggested responses". Worksheets: separate answer files. Chapter tests: answers inside the test file. All in TEACHER ONLY folders. Ch 2's suggested responses is a PDF, not a Word file.
